# Workflow

## 1. Inspect before writing

Read all supplied files and links that are necessary for the requested scope.
Separate facts from the user's desired framing. If current or unstable facts
matter, verify them with authoritative sources before drafting.

Summarize the material state:

- what is directly observed;
- what is supported by a source;
- what is the author's judgment;
- what is missing;
- what must not be claimed.

## 2. Apply the topic gate

Score the proposed article direction qualitatively on four axes:

1. **Reader value** — a defined reader receives a concrete result.
2. **Evidence strength** — central claims have code, measurements, screenshots,
   documents, or traceable sources.
3. **Cognitive tension** — the article contains a real conflict, failed path,
   tradeoff, misconception, or counterintuitive result.
4. **Leslie perspective** — the article has a defensible author judgment rather
   than a generic compilation.

Do not turn these into fake numerical precision. If evidence strength fails,
produce a research and evidence plan instead of an authoritative article.

## 3. Define the reader contract

Write one sentence containing:

- the reader;
- the problem;
- the promised outcome;
- the boundary of the article.

Write one separate thesis sentence. The reader contract is the service promise;
the thesis is Leslie's judgment.

## 4. Route the structure

Choose the primary archetype in `archetypes.md`. Avoid combining more than two
archetypes because hybrid articles often lose their main line.

For every proposed section, record:

- the question it answers;
- evidence IDs it uses;
- the takeaway or action it gives the reader;
- the visual that could reduce explanation cost.

Remove sections that do not advance the thesis or reader contract.

## 5. Draft with controlled progression

Prefer this reasoning movement when suitable:

```text
concrete friction or result
→ why the obvious explanation is incomplete
→ evidence and mechanism
→ implementation or decision
→ results and failure modes
→ transferable lesson and boundary
```

Use a short return-to-thesis sentence after a long example, historical aside,
comparison, or technical background section. The purpose is reader orientation,
not a repeated slogan.

## 6. Keep human decisions human

Ask for confirmation instead of guessing when the text depends on:

- a personal experience not in the material;
- an emotion or motive;
- a central recommendation with multiple defensible options;
- a private company or customer detail;
- permission to reveal code, screenshots, metrics, or names.

## 7. Complete through gates

Run mechanical validation first, then the four qualitative gates. Mechanical
success does not override a content failure. A draft with unresolved evidence
may be delivered as a clearly labeled draft, never as release-ready.
