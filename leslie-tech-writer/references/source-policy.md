# Evidence and source policy

## Evidence types

Use these exact ledger types:

- `experience` — Leslie directly performed or observed it.
- `experiment` — a reproducible test with conditions and results.
- `source` — a traceable external source.
- `opinion` — an attributed judgment, not presented as fact.
- `inference` — a reasoned conclusion that is explicitly labeled.
- `unusable` — hearsay, missing provenance, prohibited, or contradicted.

Use these statuses:

- `verified`
- `pending`
- `opinion`
- `unusable`

## First-person rule

First-person claims require `experience` or `experiment` evidence with explicit
permission in the ledger. Never convert a public case study into “we did” or “I
found”. If Leslie independently reproduces it, describe the reproduction and
its environment rather than borrowing the source author's identity.

## Numbers and comparisons

For every meaningful number, preserve:

- metric definition;
- test or reporting period;
- sample size where available;
- baseline;
- environment and version;
- source or artifact location.

Do not make a title more clickable by increasing precision beyond the evidence.

## Quotations and public samples

Keep direct quotations short, necessary, and attributed. Learn abstract
patterns from public articles; do not store or reproduce long passages as a
style template. Avoid copying distinctive phrases, metaphors, fixed endings,
or another author's personal anecdotes.

## Current information

Browse when versions, models, APIs, prices, laws, schedules, company roles, or
recent events could have changed. Prefer primary sources and place citations
next to supported claims. Record access date when it affects interpretation.

## Privacy and permission

Do not expose customer data, employee names, internal URLs, credentials,
private repositories, proprietary code, or screenshots without explicit scope.
Redact secrets and identifiers before adding material to the article package.
